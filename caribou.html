Case Study 01

Mobile Offer Selection Redesign

Redesigning Caribou's mobile offer-selection experience so customers could compare refinance options more confidently and understand the path from offer review to lender submission.

Before

Users faced two challenges. First, the original offer screen was designed desktop-first, and it was tough to swipe and compare our five possible offers. Second, we weren't highlighting offers like users needed us to - which one had the lowest APR? The shortest term?

After

The redesigned page is a vertical scroll that gives clear visual cues. We highlighted those 'superlatives', like lowest APR, but also critical factors like term and APR in a compact card that makes for much easier comparison.

Mobile Offer Selection

Narrative

Context

This case study focuses on the moment after customers are shown refinance offers. At this point, they need to compare loan terms, understand tradeoffs, and decide whether to continue toward lender review.

Challenge

Offer selection is a dense decision point: APR, payment, term length, savings, lender context, and next-step language all compete for attention. On mobile, that complexity is even harder to manage because the screen has less room for comparison and reassurance.

Design Approach

The redesign clarified the information hierarchy around the selected offer, reduced visual noise, and made the primary action easier to identify. Supporting details were organized so customers could scan the offer first, then dig into terms and context as needed.

What Changed

The after state turns the page from a crowded comparison moment into a more guided decision flow. The selected offer, key terms, and continuation path are easier to read, while secondary information feels less distracting.

Project Details

  • Primary Goal Help customers compare refinance offers and continue with confidence.
  • Product Moment Mobile offer selection after customers are shown refinance terms.
  • Design Lens Offer hierarchy, comparison clarity, scan order, financial confidence, and next-step comprehension.
  • Funnel Context Offer selection connects customers shown refinance offers to applications sent to lenders for review.
  • Evidence to Add Use completion, offer-selection, lender-submission, or drop-off data if available.
Case Study 02

Mobile Experience Direction

Extending the Caribou experience across smaller screens and marketing touchpoints so the product story stays clear before, during, and after the refinancing decision.

Mobile Experience

Narrative

Context

The second study focuses on how the Caribou experience translates into mobile and surrounding digital moments. Users may enter the product story from different devices, but they still need the same calm sense of direction.

Challenge

Mobile layouts have less room for explanation, comparison, and brand reassurance. The design needs to preserve priority, trust, and next-step clarity without relying on a dense desktop layout.

Design Approach

The direction uses a more focused screen composition, cleaner sequencing, and supporting brand imagery to keep the experience approachable while still pointing users toward meaningful action.

What Changed

The page now gives this work its own study instead of burying the mobile visual as one evidence card inside the offer-selection story.

Project Details

  • Primary Goal Make the Caribou experience feel coherent and readable across mobile and supporting web moments.
  • Design Lens Responsive hierarchy, scan order, visual focus, and trust-building brand cues.
  • Evidence Set Mobile redesign, website view, and hero imagery treated as one connected experience direction.
  • Outcome Focus Support a clearer path into the refinancing experience from smaller screens.

Agent Workflow

Screen Review The mobile direction was evaluated for priority, readability, and the strength of its first impression.
Context Mapping Supporting web and brand visuals were grouped around the same user entry story.
Design Synthesis Findings were organized around responsive clarity, visual focus, and product confidence.
