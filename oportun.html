Mobile App Design

In Depth

Context

Oportun had a laundry list of possibilities for mobile app features, and disagreements on all the bells and whistles. With our designer, I conducted research on what we absolutely needed, and what we could shelve. We also did research on competitors, and banks we knew were popular with our customers.

Challenge

The visibility of the project presented a big hurdle, along with the timeline. But a bigger challenge was limited access to our customers. We wanted to speak to our actual customers, and see them in person for tests, but that wasn't on the table. We had two options, and we used both: talk to our customers on the phone, and go with usertesting.com as a way to access proxy customers.

Approach

I wanted to speak to customers about what they would really need our app for - need, not want. I kept my script a little loose, since I would have no visual aids as a support. For our usertesting.com group, we had clickable prototypes to evaluate once we had a feature set from our customers.

What Changed?

Some customers told us they would still go to a store to make payments in cash, but the app would be a good option. They would want autopay, like any other bank, and they would also want to pay on demand when they needed to, or send a payment from any bank account. Oportun customers shift money between bank accounts to manage their expenses, and they need this flexibility when they make their loan payments too. Otherwise, they need from Oportun what they need from any other financial institution. Our prototype testing showed us how we could adjust our language and navigation better.

Secured Personal Loan Product Research

Oportun decided to offer a secured personal loan product to reach a broader customer base. This product would require a a vehicle as collateral, and our team was assigned to understand customers' familiarity with the concept, how Oportun could introduce it to customers who qualified, and how to support store associates with the new product.

Foothill Blvd, Oakland
Secured Personal Loan Product Research

In Depth

Context

Oportun was launching a new revenue-generating product, a secured personal loan, and ahead of launch they needed to understand how to best present it to customers both online and in-store. This would be their first product that required customer collateral. At the time, their customer base was largely Spanish speaking, with thin credit files. We needed to understand their history with these types of loans.

Challenge

The internal language we had around the product was rife with jargon, and not friendly to Spanish speakers in particular. Our best shot was in-person interviews, trying to understand whether customers had even heard the phrase 'collateral', and if they had, what it meant for them. If they hadn't, we would use the more straightforward language we hoped would work in stores. If that didn't work either, we had a good signal. This product might fall flat on its face.

Approach

We arranged visits with three store managers, who knew their customers well enough to arrange interviews. Many customers came in to make loan payments once a month, and had good relationships with the staff. We could spend half an hour to an hour chatting about their experiences, and came away with not just their perceptions of the loan product, but a look into their daily lives.

What Changed?

We had to take some burden away from store associates and call center staff. Visual aides would be critical for finding a VIN, odometer information, vehicle registration, and anything state-specific. Customers also had a fear that a single missed or late payment could lead to a repossession of their car. Any language we used about this product had to be careful, especially in translation. 'Unsecured' vs 'secured' was going to be a challenge, because the literal translation to Spanish is 'seguro' - safe.
